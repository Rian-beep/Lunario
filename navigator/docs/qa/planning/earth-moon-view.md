# Earth and Moon communications context

`EarthMoonView({catalog, timeIndex, hideControls?: boolean})` is an independent full-pane Three view. The main Moon view remains the default; the parent selects this component. `hideControls` hides its own view buttons, card and restore button. The card also has independent minimize, close and restore controls. All controls carry `data-dsn-controls`; view controls begin below the main view switch.

Earth–Moon distance retains the catalog's JPL centre range and nominal radii in kilometres. Directions use the catalog's lunar-body subearth coordinates (+X prime meridian, +Y north, −Z east). Original NASA Earth and million-triangle Moon models retain their physical size ratio. The size comparison uses the same km scale for the original NASA Sun, Earth and Moon; its spacing is explicitly a layout, not orbital distance. Camera framing adapts to viewport aspect. Display lighting and the Earth model's authored attitude are not epoch-specific Earth rotation or terminators.

Station inspection uses a separately georeferenced WGS84 ellipsoid, NASA's historical 2048×1024 Blue Marble and three enlarged markers. JPL 810-005 / 301 Rev O, Table 5 (1 July 2024), supplies the WGS84 geodetic coordinates for representative 70 m DSS-14, DSS-63 and DSS-43 antennas at Goldstone, Madrid and Canberra. These are ground complexes, not satellites. Station buttons and front-side marker clicks focus the selected region. Pins are intentionally enlarged; Earth geometry is not. Longitude registration is independent of the unregistered original Earth GLB attitude.

No station-to-Moon visibility, scheduling, service allocation or contact pass is inferred. The catalog lacks a qualified terrestrial-rotation / inertial Moon transform and local station horizons. Centre-to-centre light time is labelled separately from station path delay. A solar range helper derives Moon-based range from nominal solar radius and sampled angular semidiameter; it does not invent a heliocentric ephemeris.

Weather is fetched only after an explicit button press, from Open-Meteo's dated model forecast. UTC hourly coverage and fetch date are displayed. A selected mission epoch must lie within a returned hourly interval; present-day weather never fills a future mission date. Null is unknown rather than zero. Strict unit, coverage, sample-count and response-size checks reject malformed data. The current future-mission example therefore ordinarily says no sourced forecast covers the epoch. Offline startup makes no weather request. Weather does not certify a radio link.

For standalone delivery embed `public/data/dsn/earth-blue-marble-2048.jpg` as `window.__SOLARREACH_DSN_EARTH_TEXTURE__`; the exported normal-host path is `/data/dsn/earth-blue-marble-2048.jpg`. Missing offline imagery reports an explicit error. NASA models use the existing celestial loaders and their offline embedding. Texture provenance, credit and SHA-256 are in `public/data/dsn/provenance.json`.

Validation: six targeted numerical/weather tests, full TypeScript check and targeted ESLint pass. Tests compare WGS84 conversion against independent JPL Table 6 geocentric radii within 2 m; verify UV cardinal registration, body units, range interpolation, direction axes, angular-radius range and future-weather exclusion. Browser station focus, physical view framing, dismiss/restore and offline embedding require the parent's newly integrated build; no browser pass is claimed for this unintegrated component.

Sources: [NASA DSN](https://www.nasa.gov/communicating-with-missions/dsn/), [JPL coordinates](https://deepspace.jpl.nasa.gov/dsndocs/810-005/301/301O.pdf), [JPL DSN Now](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/deep-space-network-now/), [NASA Blue Marble](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/), [Open-Meteo units and forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs).
