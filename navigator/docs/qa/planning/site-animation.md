# Continuous site navigation and Moon atlas appearance

Site changes keep one WebGL renderer, NASA Moon/Sun/Earth/lander resources and the initial display ENU frame. Destination surface sampling and area-weighted normal accumulation yield in bounded batches; typed arrays avoid growing multi-million-element JavaScript arrays. The first original NASA Moon position/normal preparation also yields. A generation token and abort signal prevent an older site task from publishing over a newer request. Disposed tasks release geometry. Existing terrain remains visible until destination geometry and cached georeferenced imagery selections can swap atomically. Preparation status is visible and camera controls remain available, including reduced-motion selection.

Flight vectors are converted from the live simulation's site ENU and radius into the initial render frame. Readouts are projected back into live ENU. Observer horizons, site markers, axes, ground tracks and contextual camera offsets use the same transformation. The NASA globe remains in its original mounted frame. These are display transforms; numerical scenario, terrain/horizon computation and flight physics do not change. Drag/wheel cancellation stops the camera while the selected site's resource preparation may still finish; retargeting aborts obsolete resource preparation.

The separate pure camera-curve change is owned by the transition worker: log height above the destination lunar surface and longer phases replace log body-radius interpolation. Runtime does not restart the approach or remount at a phase boundary. Completion changes camera mode without applying another preset.

Default Moon appearance uses the shared linear Rec.709 luminance policy and retains source-image acquisition shadows. Atlas output bypasses scene light and ACES/exposure; camera movement cannot change source brightness. Epoch lighting is an explicit alternative, illustrative because image shadows remain baked. Coverage no longer changes texture tint. Equipment gold and NASA Sun/Earth colour remain unchanged. The equipment fill light is site-relative rather than camera-relative. Ground and the original NASA globe are mutually exclusive display meshes, avoiding overlap at the representation boundary. Different source mosaics retain real acquisition/contrast differences; this does not manufacture a common physical albedo map.

Validation before integration: focused scene tests cover fixed-frame destination location/radial normals, cooperative cancellation, existing numerical surface/coordinate invariants and actual shader compilation hooks for grayscale, atlas lighting bypass and explicit epoch mode. Full TypeScript and targeted ESLint pass with the shared appearance helper copied read-only from its owning worker for validation; it is not included in this commit. Browser camera-phase frame timing, retarget/cancel/reduced motion and screenshots require the coordinated rebuilt preview and are not yet claimed.

## Globe control limit correction

The previous globe preset incorrectly used 2.5× the maximum original mesh radius as both a body collision radius and OrbitControls target-distance minimum, and disabled pan. This prevented meaningful approach from overview and conflated panned target distance with physical altitude. The globe now keeps the camera outside the actual mesh's bounding radius plus 50 m; this conservative bound is a display collision guard, not a terrain clearance assessment. Target distance remains independently small so off-centre pan works. Mouse left-orbit/middle-dolly/right-pan and one-touch orbit/two-touch dolly-pan are explicit on initial controls and rebuilt controls. Zoom and rotation remain enabled. Regular and Ctrl-wheel behaviour still require actual browser input verification after integration.

## Geographic globe controls and sourced points

Scene props optionally accept `sites`, `selectedSiteId` and `onSelectSite(id)`. The parent supplies catalogue sites; runtime creates enlarged annotation markers at those body-fixed coordinates, hides the rear/occluded hemisphere and raycasts visible markers for hover and short-click selection. The separate selected/hovered-site card supplies an accessible explicit selection action. The original NASA geometry and numerical flight scales are unchanged.

The globe navigation component consumes live camera subpoint latitude/longitude and clockwise projected geographic-north angle. North/South camera presets and ±30° longitude steps operate independently from scenario selection, preserve body distance, use a short cancellable camera rotation and rebuild OrbitControls after the changed up direction. At an exact pole, a longitude step rotates the orientation meridian rather than inventing another physical point. The geographic panel can be minimized/dismissed/restored and is hidden in focus mode. Its position is a camera subpoint, not GPS telemetry or a lander location. Pure coordinate and component ownership is the separate geographic-navigation worker; this commit integrates those APIs only.

Candidate browser evidence before this geographic addition: served and immutable offline navigation workflows passed 8/8 assertions with no page/console errors and zero offline HTTP. All normal wheel, Ctrl-wheel, mouse and touch controls passed; site retarget/cancel preserved the exact canvas/scene and did not refetch NASA models. HTTP approach p95/max 16.8/16.8 ms; offline 16.7/116.7 ms. These measurements do not cover the new geographic UI or intentional WebGL-context release, which require the next coordinated build.

## Both-pole orbit regression

The old 0.495π polar-angle cap was a local horizon restriction applied again after site navigation. It excluded half of the camera orbit even when showing the entire Moon. All free camera contexts now allow the full polar range; physical body-radius collision remains the safety/display bound. Preset changes rebuild OrbitControls because Three caches the camera up-axis quaternion at construction. A regression test uses actual Three OrbitControls rotations and checks geographical subpoint latitude above +89.99° and below −89.99° at unchanged 5000 km radius, including a recreated controls instance after an up-axis change. Real pointer-drag verification follows the coordinated browser build.
