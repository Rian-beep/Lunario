# Reference study and audience introductions

The user requested a review of Lunar Window (Ridgelight) and NASA’s original Moon model. The reference was inspected live on 14 November 2026 at https://lunar-window-two.vercel.app/?lang=en&theme=light&t=853&site=a3-peak-near-cabeus-b .

Adopted interaction ideas using SolarReach’s own data and implementation: four-state Sun/Earth visibility breakdown, next visibility changes, clear distinction between geometry and an available radio link, and visible verification evidence with its limits. No reference metrics, coordinates, validation claims, images or code are copied. Its DSN station schedules and 18.6-year studies are separate datasets and are not claimed by this app’s 2026–2027 hourly catalog.

Each audience switch opens a dismissible guide with appropriate vocabulary, task sequence, interpretation and limits. Native dialog semantics provide focus containment and Escape dismissal; the header reopens the guide. Public explains terrain/light/contact intuition; Educator explains prediction and controlled experiments; Planner explains assumptions, constraints, comparisons and the boundary between screening and qualified mission planning.

NASA publishes original topographic Moon models at https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14959/ and rendering maps at https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/ . A detailed globe provides context; nested LOLA patches provide local measured relief. NASA explicitly documents polar artifacts in its GLB. Globe appearance never supplies flight contact, terrain hazards or visibility thresholds.

Validation includes mode switching, Escape/action dismissal, named dialogs, accessible help, pure window-state tests, preserved unknowns, and fresh numerical/source checks. Visual acceptance remains the user’s decision.
